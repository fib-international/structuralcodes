# General

Unlike the geometry of the [beam section](#theory-beam) which represents a plane cross section with stresses and strains predominantly out of the plane, the shell geometry represents a point in a plate or shell structure with stresses and strains predominantly in the plane. Wheres stress gradients over the geometry gives rise to uniaxial or biaxial bending in the beam geometry, gradients of the in-plane stresses of the shell causes plate bending moments.

We shall see that the shell section is able to represent the relation between the generalized strains $\varepsilon_{0x}$, $\varepsilon_{0y}$, $\gamma_{0xy}$, $\chi_{0x}$, $\chi_{0y}$, and $\chi_{0xy}$, i.e. the in-plane strains at the midplane and the curvatures, and the stress resultants $n_x$, $n_y$, $n_{xy}$, $m_x$, $m_y$, and $m_{xy}$, i.e. the membrane forces and the plate bending moments.
